Doral, Medley, and Hialeah Warehouse Injuries

The warehouse corridors west of the airport are full of buildings where the injured person is not an employee of the company on the sign. A delivery driver, a vendor, a wholesale customer, or an inspector can be hurt by a forklift path, a pallet in a walkway, a wet floor, or an unmarked dock.

That visitor's claim is a premises claim. An employee of the warehouse operator is often in a different system. Do not assume the two are filed the same way, and do not assume a workers' compensation sign answers the question for someone who does not work there. The full discussion is in warehouse and distribution-center injuries.

What the Floor Usually Shows

  • Forklift and pedestrian paths that share one aisle.
  • Pallets, wrap, and merchandise left in a walkway.
  • Wet polished concrete from a roof leak or cooler condensate. In a customer-facing area, a spill can also implicate section 768.0755.
  • Unmarked loading-dock edges and transitions between the warehouse slab and the office.

Doral, Medley, and Hialeah do not have a separate premises statute. The geography matters because these buildings are where outside drivers and visitors are actually sent, and because the operator, the landlord, a staffing company, and a forklift contractor may be different entities with different policies. Commercial property claims and stores and buildings explain how that split is investigated.

Fault and Deadlines

For negligence claims arising after March 24, 2023, the general filing period is two years under section 95.11. Dock video is often on a short overwrite cycle. Photograph the aisle, keep the shoes if they are part of what happened, and get medical care before anyone argues that a later appointment means a later injury.

Comparative fault under section 768.81 includes whether you left a marked pedestrian path that actually existed. If the only place to walk was the forklift aisle, the photos should show that. Being more than 50 percent at fault bars recovery.

What This Page Does Not Cover

This is not a claim about a defective product inside a box, and it is not a substitute for an employee's workers' compensation question. It is about people invited onto the property who are hurt by the way the property is run. If the injury happened on a public road outside the gate, or on city property, section 768.28 may apply instead. Bring the address. The gate and the public street are not the same defendant.
