Apartment, Condo, and Rental Injuries

Part of the Miami-Dade premises injury library from Recalde Law Firm, P.A.

Call 305-792-9100 Free Consultation

Most residential injuries that lead to a premises claim happen outside the four walls of a leased unit: stairwells, parking decks, pool decks, mailrooms, and walkways. The lease, the declaration, and the management contract decide who was supposed to maintain that space.

Section 83.51, Florida Statutes, requires landlords to comply with applicable building, housing, and health codes and, where it applies, to keep common areas clean and safe and structural components in good repair. A condominium association's duty usually follows the declaration and which elements are common property. A short-term rental adds the host, the owner, and sometimes a local manager. The booking platform is rarely the party that fixes the railing.

For crimes in multifamily buildings with at least five units, section 768.0706 can give an owner a presumption against liability if listed security measures were substantially implemented. That presumption is a defense. It is not a map of every apartment injury, and it does not apply to an ordinary fall on a broken step.
