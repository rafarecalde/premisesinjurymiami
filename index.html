First 48 Hours

The Condition Gets Fixed. The Video Gets Taped Over.

For most private-property negligence claims that arose after March 24, 2023, the filing deadline is two years under section 95.11. Surveillance is often overwritten in days.

  1. Photograph the hazard

    Wide shots and close-ups, before anyone mops, patches, or ropes it off. Keep the shoes you wore.

  2. Get the incident report

    Ask for a copy. Write down the name of the manager you told and the time you reported it.

  3. Get medical care

    An insurer will argue that a delay means the injury came from somewhere else.

  4. Do not give a recorded statement

    The property insurer may call sounding helpful. Talk to a lawyer before you record anything.

Florida Rules

The Statutes That Change the Case

Florida law requires the person who controls property to use reasonable care for invited visitors and to warn of hidden dangers. These are the rules that come up on the first call. The Florida law library goes deeper.

Spills

768.0755

A business-floor spill requires proof the business knew or should have known about the substance.

 Fault

768.81

Recovery is barred when the injured person is more than 50 percent at fault.

 Government

768.28

Cities, the county, and other public property add notice rules, a waiting period, and caps.

 Deadline

95.11

Most negligence claims that arose after March 24, 2023, have two years to file.

More than one company can share responsibility: the owner, the property manager, a commercial tenant, a condominium association, a maintenance contractor, or a contractor whose work creates a hazard for people passing by. Section 768.0706 is a security presumption for certain multifamily buildings. It does not apply to stores or nightclubs. Fatal injuries are addressed under the Wrongful Death Act, sections 768.16 through 768.26. Dog bites on rental property are addressed under section 767.04.

Depending on the facts, a Florida premises claim can include medical expenses, lost wages, loss of earning capacity, and pain and suffering. Every case turns on the injury, the proof, comparative fault, and the insurance that applies. See how damages work and how coverage is layered.
