Miami Beach Hotel, Pool, and Nightlife Injuries

Hotels, pool decks, restaurant floors, and late-night venues sit on a barrier island. Salt air, sudden rain, and crowded sidewalks are the conditions people describe. The Florida rules are statewide. The property is what changes.

Guests are invited onto hotel and resort property. The operator owes them reasonable care to keep the premises reasonably safe and to warn of hidden dangers the operator knows about or should find by a reasonable inspection. A puddle in a lobby is not analyzed the same way as a broken deck drain or a railing that has been loose for months.

Hotels, Lobbies, and Pool Decks

Miami Beach and Sunny Isles towers show the same split that hotel and pool claims turn on. A spill inside a hotel restaurant can fall under section 768.0755, which requires proof the business knew or should have known about a transitory substance. A cracked deck, a missing drain cover, or a guard corroded by salt air is a condition of the property. That knowledge rule for spills does not replace the duty to maintain the building.

Pool-deck falls, diving injuries, drownings, and drain entrapment are covered in the pool injury guide and the pool-safety rules guide. Balcony and railing falls, including guards that loosen in coastal air, are covered here. More than one company may be involved: the owner, a brand manager, and a maintenance contractor do not always share one insurance policy. The hotel and resort guide walks through that split.

Valet lanes and porte-cocheres along Collins and at resort entries raise a different question: who controlled the handoff lane, and was the path the stand sent you on a safe place to walk. See valet injuries.

Restaurants, Bars, and Nightclubs

South Beach rooms overwrite cameras quickly. A receipt, a wristband, and a short phone video are often what is left by the next afternoon. Spills, broken seating, and crowds inside a restaurant or bar are premises questions. Florida's dram-shop statute, section 768.125, is narrow and is not the same claim as a wet floor or a blocked exit.

Section 768.0706, the security presumption for certain multifamily buildings, does not cover nightclubs or stores. Overcrowding, staffing, and lighting at a venue are discussed in the restaurant and nightlife pages and in nightclub overcrowding.

Sidewalks and City Property

A City of Miami Beach sidewalk, park path, or other government-controlled surface can bring section 768.28: written notice, a waiting period, and damages caps. A slab in front of a hotel is not automatically the city's. The abutting owner, a tenant, or a contractor may control it. The sidewalk guide is the place to sort that before anyone assumes there is only one defendant.

What to Preserve

Photograph the floor, deck, or rail before it is mopped or repaired. Keep the shoes. Ask for the incident report. Get witness names before people leave the property. For most private-property negligence claims that arose after March 24, 2023, section 95.11 allows two years to file. Video is often gone in days. Do not give a recorded statement to the property insurer before you have talked with a lawyer. Comparative fault under section 768.81 can reduce or bar recovery above 50 percent, which is one reason the photos matter.

Hurt on Property in Miami?

Photograph the condition, keep your shoes, and call before the video is overwritten. Free consultation.
