Hurt in a Miami Rideshare or Taxi Pickup Zone

By Rafael Recalde, Esq. • Recalde Law Firm, P.A. • October 29, 2026

The last few yards of a ride in Miami are a designed space: a hotel porte-cochere, a condo overhang, a mall curb, a bar's valet-adjacent lane, or the airport. People fall on broken curbs, unmarked steps from the lobby down to the drive, and standing water. They are also struck while waiting where the app told them to stand. Those are not all the same lawsuit.

The Property and the Driver

A claim about the curb, the lighting, the step, or a pickup lane with no safe place to stand is a premises claim against whoever maintained that property. The hotel, the condominium, the mall, or a government airport authority is the starting point. A driver who hits a person standing in that lane may be an auto claim against the driver and, depending on the trip, against the company's commercial coverage. The rideshare company is not automatically the owner of the curb, and a premises theory should not be written as if it were.

Hotels and condos in Brickell and on Miami Beach often share this geometry: a covered drive, a slick tile landing, and cars that cannot see around a column. If the stand or the "wait here" sign puts guests in the driver's blind spot, the layout itself is the condition. Valet lanes raise the same layout question and are covered separately in the valet guide.

Airports and Other Government Curbs

Miami International Airport's roadways are a government setting. Section 768.28, Florida Statutes, can apply to the authority that controls the curb, including written notice before suit, a waiting period, and damages caps. A private company operating a portion of the commercial lane may be an additional defendant outside that statute. PortMiami's terminal curbs raise the same split and are discussed in the cruise-terminal guide.

A city sidewalk at the pin the app dropped is a classic sidewalk case: identify whether the city, the county, or the adjacent private owner maintains that slab before you assume who to notify.

What to Save From the Phone

  • A screenshot of the trip request showing the time and the pin, before the app drops the trip from view.
  • Photos of the curb, the step, the column, and the lighting. Take them that night if darkness is the point.
  • The door or stand name. "Front entrance" is not enough in a hotel with three.
  • A police report if a vehicle made contact. Property incident reports and crash reports are different documents.
  • The shoes, and medical care the same day.

Two insurers will call. The auto insurer and the property insurer do not have the same file. A recorded statement to one of them can be used by both. You can report the facts of where you were standing without arguing fault on a recording.

Private-property negligence claims arising after March 24, 2023, generally have two years under section 95.11. Government curbs add section 768.28. Comparative fault under section 768.81 includes whether you stood in a travel lane when a marked waiting area existed. If the marked area was itself the hazard, that fact belongs in the photos.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is the rideshare company responsible for the curb?

Not automatically. A claim about a broken curb, a dark porte-cochere, or a pickup lane with nowhere to stand is usually a premises claim against whoever designed and maintained that property. A driver who strikes a pedestrian may face a separate auto claim. Those are different cases.

Q: What about Miami International Airport?

Airport roadways and curbs are often controlled by a government authority. Section 768.28, Florida Statutes, can apply, including written notice before suit. A private concessionaire operating part of the curb may be a separate party.

Q: What evidence is specific to a pickup-zone injury?

A screenshot of the ride request with the time and pin, photos of the curb and the lighting, the door or stand where you were told to wait, and a police report if a vehicle was involved.
