Hurt Visiting a Miami School or Daycare?

By Rafael Recalde, Esq. • Recalde Law Firm, P.A. • October 29, 2026

Schools and daycares are busy twice a day. Parents, grandparents, and caregivers cross wet walkways, uneven parking stops, and lobbies that were just mopped. A fall in that setting is usually a premises claim about the campus. It is not a claim about how a teacher handled a classroom, and it should not be described that way.

Visitor Falls and Children's Injuries Are Different Cases

If you were on campus to drop off or pick up a child and you fell, the questions are ordinary premises questions. Was the floor wet without a warning? Was a wheel stop in the car line broken? Was the evening-event path dark? Who maintains that piece of ground: the school, a church that owns the building, a management company, or a district?

If a child was hurt on playground equipment, the facts shift toward surfacing, broken parts, and supervision. Those issues are covered in the playground guide. A visiting adult's broken wrist and a child's injury on a climber can happen on the same campus and still be different claims, against different duties.

Public Schools Use the Government Rules

Miami-Dade County public schools are government defendants. Section 768.28, Florida Statutes, requires a written notice of claim, imposes a waiting period, and caps what the agency pays. Notice sent to the school office, and never to the right agency, can waste the time the statute allows. The notice-of-claim guide walks through those steps.

A private daycare, a church preschool, or a learning center in a strip mall is usually a private premises claim. The landlord of the shopping center may also control the parking lot and the sidewalk at the door, which is the same split described in commercial property cases. A charter school or a campus in a leased building can involve both a public operator and a private owner. Ask who employs the staff and who owns the dirt.

Hazards That Show Up at Arrival and Dismissal

  • Lobby tile mopped during car line, with the wet-floor sign facing the office instead of the door.
  • Rain mats that have curled and become the trip hazard.
  • Parking stops, speed bumps, and unmarked drops between the asphalt and the walkway.
  • Gates and door closers that slam on hands, which is a maintenance item if the hardware was already failing.
  • Evening performances and games, when lighting the path back to the lot is part of the event the school invited people to attend.
  • Playground surfaces and equipment if the injured person is a child. Heat on metal and plastic is a Miami-specific version of the same problem.

Do not assume a child's claim waits until age 18. Florida tolls a minor's negligence claim only in narrow situations, including under section 95.051 when the child has no parent or guardian or the parent's interests conflict, and even then there is an outer limit. The playground guide states that rule. The general period for negligence claims arising after March 24, 2023, is two years under section 95.11.

Records Worth Asking For

The school will have an incident report if someone insisted on one. Ask for it the same day and keep a photo of the floor or the parking surface before a cone appears. Visitor logs, after-school sign-out sheets, and any work order about the same leak or the same light are useful later. If the campus is public, preserving those records still matters, and the notice letter under section 768.28 is a separate task with its own deadline.

Comparative fault under section 768.81 is raised when a parent was looking at a phone in car line or stepping over an obvious cone. Being more than 50 percent at fault bars recovery. A photo of a clear puddle with no sign is how that argument is answered.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: I fell picking up my child. Is that a premises claim?

A visitor's fall on a wet lobby floor, a broken walkway, or a poorly lit parking area is generally a premises claim about the condition of the property. It is a different case from a claim about how a child was supervised in class.

Q: Are public schools treated like private daycares?

Often no. Miami-Dade County public school property is typically a government claim under section 768.28, Florida Statutes, which requires written notice and imposes damages caps. A private daycare or a church-run preschool is usually a private premises claim. A charter or a leased campus can involve both the operator and the building owner.

Q: Does a child have extra time to file?

Families should not count on that. Florida tolls a minor's claim only in narrow situations, discussed in the playground guide, including section 95.051. The usual negligence deadline for claims that arose after March 24, 2023, is two years under section 95.11.
