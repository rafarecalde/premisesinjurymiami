Injured by Debris or a Dark Property After a Miami Storm

By Rafael Recalde, Esq. • Recalde Law Firm, P.A. • October 29, 2026

After a storm, Miami properties are full of new conditions: limbs on walkways, pavers that shifted, balcony furniture in the wrong place, pool fences down, and stairwells dark because backup lighting failed. People are also hurt indoors days later, when a leak that started during the storm is still putting water on tile. A storm explains how the hazard arrived. It does not, by itself, decide who had to deal with it afterward.

Reasonable Care Has a Clock, and the Clock Is Factual

The person who controls a property still has to act reasonably once conditions allow. That can include clearing a walkway they maintain, warning guests and tenants about a hazard they know of, and restoring lighting they are responsible for. How quickly that must happen depends on access, whether power is out to the building, and how obvious the danger is. A hidden hole under ordinary landscaping the week after a storm is a different fact from a street the city has not yet reached.

Do not assume a storm creates a special immunity, and do not assume every post-storm injury is the owner's fault. Comparative fault under section 768.81 is a real issue if you walked through an obvious debris field. Recovery is barred if you are found more than 50 percent at fault. An obvious pile of branches and a dark interior stair that residents were still expected to use are not the same choice.

Where These Injuries Happen

  • Private walkways and condo entrances, where the association or the manager controls the path. See the condo guide and the apartment guide.
  • Stairwells that go dark when a building's emergency lighting does not come on.
  • Interior floors wet from a roof or window leak. If the water sits long enough to grow mold, the mold and water-damage guide becomes relevant alongside the fall.
  • Pool gates and fences that are no longer closed, which connects to the pool injury guide.
  • Public sidewalks and parks. Those can be government claims under section 768.28, with written notice and damages caps. Identifying the right agency is discussed in the sidewalk guide.

The property policy is not the liability policy. An insurer paying to tarp a roof is not the same company, and not the same coverage, as the liability carrier for a later fall. Do not treat a claim number for building damage as a claim number for an injury.

Deadlines and Proof

The negligence period generally runs from the date of the injury, not from landfall. Claims arising after March 24, 2023, generally have two years under section 95.11. Cleanup is fast. A branch pile that caused a fall on Thursday may be chipped on Friday. Photograph it, note the date of the storm and the date of the injury separately, and keep any message to the manager asking for lights or for a cleanup. Those messages are the knowledge evidence.

If you were hurt on a private balcony by storm-shifted furniture or a guard the wind exposed as already loose, the balcony and railing guide is the closer fit. The storm may be how you discovered the condition. The maintenance history is how you prove it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Does a hurricane erase the property owner's duty?

A storm does not, by itself, decide the claim. After conditions allow, the person who controls the property still has to act reasonably about hazards they know of on areas they maintain, including warnings and lighting they are responsible for. How fast that must happen depends on access, power, and how obvious the danger was.

Q: Can I be blamed for walking through debris?

Comparative fault under section 768.81 can be raised if you were more careless than the law allows, and recovery is barred if you are found more than 50 percent at fault. An obvious debris field and a hidden hole under ordinary landscaping are not the same facts.

Q: When does the deadline start?

For a negligence claim, the period generally runs from the injury, not from the day the storm made landfall. Claims that arose after March 24, 2023, generally have two years under section 95.11. Government property, such as a county park or a city sidewalk, follows section 768.28 as well.
