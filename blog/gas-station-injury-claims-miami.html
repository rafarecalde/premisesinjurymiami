Injured at a Miami Gas Station or Convenience Store

By Rafael Recalde, Esq. • Recalde Law Firm, P.A. • October 29, 2026

A Miami gas station is several properties in one lot: the forecourt, the convenience-store floor, the air and vacuum pad, the car-wash exit, and the edge of the sidewalk at the street. People fall on fuel, on drinks, on torn mats, and on pump islands that have crumbled. At night, some of the same lots are where assaults happen. Each injury follows the party who controlled that piece of the property.

Spills and Permanent Defects

Fuel on concrete, a slush spilled by the door, or a mop bucket trail inside the store is a transitory foreign substance at a business. Section 768.0755, Florida Statutes, requires proof of actual or constructive knowledge. How long the fuel sat there, whether attendants crossed it, and whether anyone had a practice of walking the islands are the proof. A raised curb, a broken pump-island nose, or a mat that has been torn for weeks is a condition of the premises. The spill statute does not replace the duty to fix it.

The same split appears in grocery stores. The difference at a station is the product. Fuel is an expected liquid in a place where customers are also walking. That does not make a puddle reasonable. It makes inspection part of operating the business.

The Name on the Canopy Is Not Always the Operator

Many stations are owned by a landlord, leased to a dealer, and branded by a fuel company that may or may not direct day-to-day safety. A cleaning crew may be a fourth company. Responsibility follows control: who scheduled the walks of the forecourt, who was supposed to repair the island, and who employed the cashier. The brand's advertising does not, by itself, answer that. Contracts do.

If the fall was in a parking space the station shares with a neighboring store, look at the lot lines. Commercial properties in Miami often share access drives. The commercial property guide covers that overlap.

Nighttime Assaults

An assault at the pumps or at the side of the building can be a negligent security claim: lighting that is out, cameras that were decorative, a history of incidents, or a lot with no sight line from the register. The multifamily presumption in section 768.0706 does not apply to a gas station. Section 768.0701 can require a jury to weigh the fault of the person who committed the crime. The criminal case does not replace the premises claim or extend its deadline.

Photograph the pump number. Fuel is washed off quickly. A photo of the island, the receipt with the time, and the shoes you wore are what is left after the next car pulls in.

Practical Steps

  • Report the fall to the attendant and ask for a manager and an incident report.
  • Photograph the substance or the defect before it is cleaned or coned.
  • Keep the shoes and, if fuel is on clothing, bag the clothing.
  • Get medical care the same day. Fuel and concrete injuries are easy for an insurer to call "minor" if you wait.
  • If it is an assault, ask for a police report number before you leave.

Negligence claims arising after March 24, 2023, generally must be filed within two years under section 95.11. Comparative fault under section 768.81, including a claim that you were looking at a phone at the pump, reduces or can bar recovery if your share is more than 50 percent. The condition of the island is how that percentage is contested.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is a fuel spill on the forecourt covered by section 768.0755?

A transitory substance at a business open to customers, including fuel or a drink spilled on the forecourt or inside the store, is the kind of condition section 768.0755 addresses. The injured person must prove the business had actual or constructive knowledge. A broken pump island or a raised curb is a permanent condition, not a spill.

Q: Is the fuel brand responsible?

Sometimes the brand does not operate the station. Liability depends on who controlled the area and the safety practices there: the dealer, a landlord, a cleaning contractor, or, in some arrangements, a company that directed day-to-day operations. The name on the canopy is the start of that question, not the end.

Q: What if the injury was an assault at night?

That may be a negligent security claim about lighting, staffing, or prior incidents. The multifamily presumption in section 768.0706 does not apply to a gas station.
