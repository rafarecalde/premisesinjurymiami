Injured on a Miami Marina, Dock, or Pier?

By Rafael Recalde, Esq. • Recalde Law Firm, P.A. • October 29, 2026

Miami's marinas are working surfaces that guests treat like sidewalks. People walk finger piers at Dinner Key, Coconut Grove, Miami Beach, and along condo docks to reach a restaurant, a boat, or a slip they rented for the weekend. The boards are wet, the lighting is uneven, and salt air eats hardware. A fall there is often a premises claim against whoever controlled the dock, not automatically a maritime case.

The Dock and the Boat Are Different Places

A guest who trips on a marina walkway, a finger pier, or the ramp from a parking lot down to the docks is usually suing the marina, the yacht club, the restaurant tenant, the condominium association, or a public owner. The duty is reasonable care for people invited onto that property. A claim for something that happened on board a vessel — a ladder, a deck fitting, a captain's operation — can be a different case with different rules. Say precisely where your foot was when you fell.

Public marinas operated by a city or by Miami-Dade County can be government defendants. Section 768.28 then requires written notice and sets a waiting period and damages caps. A private club or a condo dock does not. The sign at the gate is the first clue, not the last. Management companies run many "public-looking" marinas under contract with a private owner.

What Fails on a Miami Dock

  • Boards that are soft, missing, or replaced with a plate that sits above the walking line.
  • Gaps between finger piers and the main walkway that are wider than a person expects at low tide or after a repair.
  • Cleats, hoses, and shore-power cords left across the walkway.
  • Ramps and gangways to the dock with no usable handrail.
  • Lighting that leaves the outer slips dark while the restaurant patio is bright.
  • Algae or fish-cleaning water on a ramp. A spill in a marina store may be a transitory substance under section 768.0755. A rotten plank is not.

Storms and king tides move this equipment. After a storm, a dock that was safe in June may have a loose section in October. That timing issue is discussed in the post-storm guide. Deferred maintenance is still a choice. Salt corrosion is foreseeable on the bay.

Photograph at the time of day you fell. A dock that looks ordinary at noon can be a different place after sunset, which is when many restaurant guests walk it. A daytime photo alone can understate the lighting problem.

Who to Name, and What to Keep

Report the fall to the dockmaster or the manager and ask for a written report. Note whether you were a slip customer, a restaurant guest, or a visitor to a boat. Those statuses affect whether you were invited onto the pier, which is discussed generally in the visitor-status guide. Keep your shoes. If a board broke under you, do not assume the marina will keep the pieces.

Insurance on marinas is often split between a property policy, a marina operator's liability policy, and a tenant's policy for the restaurant at the head of the dock. Identifying all three is the same exercise described in how coverage works. For private defendants, negligence claims arising after March 24, 2023, generally have two years under section 95.11. Government marinas add the section 768.28 notice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is every dock injury a maritime case?

Not necessarily. A guest who falls on a dock, finger pier, or marina walkway controlled by a marina, yacht club, restaurant, or condominium is often bringing a Florida premises claim against whoever controlled that surface. A claim for something that happened aboard a vessel can be different.

Q: Does the spill statute apply to a broken dock board?

Usually no. Section 768.0755 applies to transitory foreign substances in a business, such as a spill in a marina store. A rotten plank, a missing light, or a permanent gap is a condition of the property.

Q: What if the marina is public?

City and county marinas can be government defendants. Section 768.28 then requires written notice and sets caps and a waiting period. A private yacht club or condo dock is a private claim.
