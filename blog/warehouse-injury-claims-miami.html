Hurt Visiting a Miami Warehouse or Distribution Center

By Rafael Recalde, Esq. • Recalde Law Firm, P.A. • October 29, 2026

Doral, Medley, and Hialeah are full of distribution buildings where the people who get hurt are not always employees of the company on the sign. A delivery driver employed by someone else, a vendor, a customer of a wholesale counter, or an inspector can be injured by a forklift path, a pallet in a walkway, a wet floor under a roof leak, or an unmarked dock edge. That visitor's claim is a premises claim. An employee of the warehouse operator is often in a different system.

Employees and Visitors Are Not the Same

If you were employed by the company that controls the building, an injury on the job is often handled through workers' compensation against that employer. A premises claim may still exist against a separate landlord, a different tenant, or a contractor who created the hazard and does not employ you. Which path applies depends on the paycheck and on who controlled the condition. Do not assume the two are interchangeable, and do not assume workers' compensation is the only paper that can be filed.

If you were invited in to deliver, to repair, or to buy, you are generally the kind of visitor a business owes reasonable care, including a reasonably safe path. Visitor categories are explained in the invitee, guest, and trespasser guide. A person who wanders into a racking aisle that was closed off is in a weaker position than a driver who was directed across the dock by the warehouse staff.

Conditions That Produce These Claims

  • Forklifts and pedestrians sharing an aisle with no marked walkway.
  • Shrink wrap, banding, and pallets left in the path a visitor was told to use.
  • Loading-dock edges and levelers without a warning at the drop.
  • Roof leaks and cooler condensate on polished concrete. In a customer-facing area, a spill can also implicate section 768.0755.
  • Merchandise that falls from racks the visitor did not load.
  • Poor lighting on the yard between the guard shack and the office, which is the same problem as a dark garage, just outdoors.

Several Companies, One Dock Door

Warehouse buildings in west Miami-Dade are often owned by a landlord, leased to a logistics company, and serviced by separate cleaning, racking, and yard-management vendors. The name the guard writes on your visitor badge may not be the name on the lease or the name on the forklift. Write down all three. The property-management guide explains why that split matters for insurance.

Construction next door raises a related issue. A visitor hurt by materials staged in a shared drive may have a claim that looks like the construction-site visitor cases, even though the injury was "at a warehouse."

Report the injury to the site, not only to your own dispatcher. Your employer’s incident form does not require the warehouse to keep video of its dock. Ask the site supervisor to preserve the cameras and to note the aisle or door number.

For negligence claims arising after March 24, 2023, the general filing period is two years under section 95.11. Video of a dock is often on a short overwrite cycle. Photograph the condition, keep the safety shoes if they are part of the story, and get medical care before the defense argues that a later appointment means a later injury. Comparative fault under section 768.81, including a failure to follow a marked path that actually existed, can reduce or bar recovery above 50 percent.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: I was making a delivery. Is this workers' compensation or a premises claim?

If you were employed by the company that controls the warehouse, an on-the-job injury is often handled through workers' compensation against that employer. A premises claim may still exist against a different owner, tenant, or contractor who controlled the hazard. Which path applies depends on who employed you.

Q: What hazards matter in these buildings?

Forklift and pedestrian paths that share one aisle, pallets and wrap in walkways, wet floors from roof leaks, unmarked loading-dock edges, and falling merchandise. Customer-facing spills can also implicate section 768.0755.

Q: Why do Doral and Medley warehouses raise extra questions?

Buildings there are often occupied by a logistics tenant, owned by a separate landlord, and serviced by outside cleaning or racking vendors. The name on the dock door and the name on the lease are not always the same company.
