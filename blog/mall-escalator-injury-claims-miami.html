Escalator Injuries Inside Miami Malls

By Rafael Recalde, Esq. • Recalde Law Firm, P.A. • October 29, 2026

Miami malls move people on escalators that also move shopping bags, strollers, sandals, and rainwater from the parking garage. The injuries cluster in a few patterns: a shoe or a toe caught at the comb plate, clothing or a foot drawn into the skirt at the side, a sudden stop that throws riders down the steps, and a slip on the landing where the escalator meets a wet floor. Those patterns do not all have the same defendant or the same statute.

Who Maintains the Machine

A mall escalator is rarely "the store's" in any simple sense. The mall owner, the management company, and a contractor under a maintenance agreement usually sit on top of the equipment. A department store that has its own escalator inside the demising line may be the tenant's responsibility instead. The contract, not the directory, answers it. The broader ownership split is described in the mall injury guide. Elevators in the same buildings raise related maintenance issues and are covered in the elevator and escalator guide.

Florida requires elevators and escalators to be inspected and maintained. The certificate posted at the unit, the maintenance logs, and the record of prior entrapments or emergency stops are the documents that show whether that happened. A sudden stop with no later explanation is not a mystery if the logs still exist. They often do not, unless someone asks for them promptly.

Entrapment Versus a Wet Landing

A mechanical injury — a comb that grabs a shoe, a skirt that pulls a foot, a stop that throws people forward — is proved with the machine's condition and the service history. Keep the shoe and the clothing. They are the physical evidence of where the machine took hold.

A fall on water at the landing plate can instead be a transitory substance in a business under section 768.0755. Rain from a Miami garage entrance, a planter overflow, or a cleaner’s mop are typical sources. Knowledge is proved by time on the floor and by whether employees passed it. One incident can involve both a wet landing and a bad stop. Describe them separately so the proof is not blended into a single vague "the escalator was dangerous."

Note the exact unit. "The escalator by the food court" is ambiguous in a two-level mall. The store names on either side, the direction of travel, and a photo of the inspection certificate identify the machine and the contractor.

Children and Comparative Fault

Children are frequent escalator patients because sandals, untied laces, and small shoes meet the comb plate at a bad angle. The defense will talk about parental supervision and about section 768.81's comparative fault bar, which denies recovery above 50 percent fault. Whose conduct is actually compared — the mall's maintenance, the rider's, a parent's — depends on the facts and on who the claimant is. It is not a reason to skip medical care or to skip the incident report the mall security office can write the same hour.

Negligence claims arising after March 24, 2023, generally have two years under section 95.11. A mall that says the video "recycles" is describing a choice about retention. A prompt request is how that choice is tested. Get the security report number before you leave, and get medical care the same day so a later gap is not the center of the case.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Who is sued after a mall escalator injury?

Often more than one party: the mall owner, the company that manages the mall, the contractor hired to maintain the escalator, and sometimes a store if the unit serves only that tenant. The maintenance contract and the inspection certificate are the documents that sort this out.

Q: Is a slip on water at the landing an escalator defect?

Not always. Water tracked onto the landing plate can be a transitory substance under section 768.0755. A comb plate that grabs a shoe, a side skirt that pulls a foot in, or a sudden stop is a mechanical and maintenance question. Both can happen in the same incident, and they are proved differently.

Q: How is this different from an elevator case?

The duty to inspect and maintain is similar, and Florida requires elevators and escalators to be inspected and maintained. Escalator injuries more often involve entrapment at the comb or the skirt, shopping bags and shoes as evidence, and falls of several steps rather than a cab that drops or stops between floors.
