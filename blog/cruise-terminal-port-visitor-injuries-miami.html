Injured at PortMiami or a Cruise Terminal?

By Rafael Recalde, Esq. • Recalde Law Firm, P.A. • October 29, 2026

PortMiami moves an enormous number of passengers across a small amount of pavement: terminal floors, gangways at the building, baggage halls, shuttle curbs, and parking garages. A fall in that space is a premises question about whoever controlled the surface. It is a different question from an injury that happens after you have boarded a ship.

People get hurt in a few repeatable ways. Tile at the terminal entrance is wet from rain or from air-conditioning drip. A transition strip between the curb and the lobby is loose. A handrail is missing on a short flight of steps near baggage claim. A garage ramp is dark, or oil has sat on the walking path between the elevator and the cars. A concession counter leaves a spill in a food line. Each of those is a condition of property someone was paid to maintain.

Shore Property and the Ship Are Not the Same Case

If you have not boarded, start with the terminal. PortMiami is a Miami-Dade County enterprise. Private terminal operators, cruise lines, baggage handlers, cleaning contractors, and garage operators may also control pieces of the same trip from the curb to the gangway. The names on the doors and on the incident report are the list to write down. A county defendant brings section 768.28, Florida Statutes, into the case: written notice before suit, a waiting period, and damages caps. A private operator in the same building may be outside that statute. Both can be investigated at once.

Once a passenger is aboard a cruise ship, maritime law and the ticket contract often take over. Those contracts commonly shorten the time to sue for a shipboard injury. Florida's two-year negligence period in section 95.11, for claims that arose after March 24, 2023, should not be assumed to govern what happened on the vessel. Tell the lawyer which side of the gangway you were on.

Spills Versus Permanent Defects

A drink spilled in a terminal shop or restaurant is a transitory foreign substance in a business. Section 768.0755 requires proof that the business had actual or constructive knowledge of it. Constructive knowledge can be shown by how long the substance was there. A broken tile, a missing nose on a step, or a ramp with no contrast is not a spill. It is a condition of the premises, and the proof is inspection and repair history rather than a sweep log for that hour.

Write down the terminal. PortMiami has more than one terminal, and the operator named on the wall is not always Miami-Dade County. The incident report should match the building you were actually in.

What to Do Before You Leave the Port

  • Photograph the exact surface, including a wider shot that shows the absence of a warning cone or a handrail.
  • Ask security or guest services for an incident report and keep the number.
  • Get names of people who saw the fall, including crew who may fly out that night.
  • Keep the shoes. Do not wash off a substance you slipped on.
  • Get medical care the same day so the injury is tied to the fall in a record.
  • If a vehicle was involved at the curb or in the garage, ask whether a police report will be written.

Deadlines Depend on the Defendant

A private terminal contractor is generally on the two-year negligence clock in section 95.11 for claims arising after March 24, 2023. A county or other government defendant is on the notice timetable in section 768.28, which is not the same paperwork and not the same cap. A shipboard injury may be shorter still because of the ticket. Waiting to see how the cruise line's guest-claims office responds can spend the time you needed for the shore claim.

Comparative fault under section 768.81 will be raised if you were looking at a boarding pass, pulling a large bag, or walking through an obvious wet area. Being partly at fault reduces a recovery. Being more than 50 percent at fault can bar it. Photos that show why the condition was hard to see matter for that argument.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is a fall inside a PortMiami terminal a Florida premises claim?

An injury on terminal property, before you board a ship, is often analyzed as a Florida premises claim against whoever controlled that space. If the responsible party is Miami-Dade County or another government entity, section 768.28, Florida Statutes, adds written notice rules, a waiting period, and damages caps.

Q: Does Florida's two-year deadline apply once I am on the cruise ship?

Do not assume it does. For most Florida negligence claims that arose after March 24, 2023, section 95.11 allows two years. An injury aboard a cruise ship can be governed by maritime law and the ticket contract, which often sets a shorter time to sue. The terminal claim and the ship claim should be looked at separately.

Q: What should I preserve after a terminal fall?

Photograph the condition before it is cleaned or repaired, ask for an incident report, keep your shoes, note the terminal name on the signage, and write down witness names. Cameras in a busy terminal are not kept forever.
