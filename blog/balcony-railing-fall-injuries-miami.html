Balcony and Railing Falls at Miami Hotels, Condos, and Rentals

By Rafael Recalde, Esq. • Recalde Law Firm, P.A. • October 29, 2026

Salt air, wind-driven rain, and deferred paint are ordinary facts of a Miami balcony. The injuries are not ordinary. People fall when a guard is loose, when glass is missing, when a sliding door track grabs a foot, or when furniture has been pushed against a rail that was never meant to be a ladder. Hotels, condominiums, apartments, and short-term rentals all have these openings. Who must repair them is a document question, not a view question.

Hotels

A guest is invited onto the property, including the balcony of the room the hotel rented. The operator owes reasonable care. On Miami Beach and in Sunny Isles, corrosion at the base of a rail is something a maintenance program either tracks or ignores. The brand on the towel, the owner of the tower, and the company that manages the hotel may be three defendants. That structure is laid out in the hotel and resort guide. Photograph the guard, the fasteners, and any furniture against it before engineering replaces the panel.

Condominiums

Condo balconies are often limited common elements. The declaration says whether the association maintains the structure and whether the unit owner maintains the coating or the slider. A fall does not rewrite that document. The association's insurance and the unit owner's policy are both worth identifying, which is the same exercise as other condo common-area claims. Prior engineering reports after milestone inspections, if they exist for the building, are evidence of what the association had already been told about concrete and rail anchors. Ask for them by the building's address, not by a rumor about a different tower.

Apartments and Short-Term Rentals

Section 83.51, Florida Statutes, requires landlords to comply with applicable codes and, where the statute applies, to keep structural components in good repair and common areas clean and safe. A private balcony has to be matched to the right subsection and to the lease. A repair request that was ignored is as important as the photo of the loose post.

On a short-term rental, the owner, the host, and a local manager may all have had the key. The booking platform generally does not maintain the guard. The vacation-rental guide covers the insurance gaps that show up when a house was never set up as a hotel. A balcony on that house is the same problem with a higher consequence: listings that advertise the roof deck should be saved as screenshots, because the listing changes.

Do not guess a code height from memory. Whether a guard was too low, too weak, or simply detached is proved with measurements, photos, and the building's own records. A round number repeated online is not a substitute for those records.

If the Fall Is Fatal

Balcony falls are among the premises injuries that become wrongful death claims. Who may bring the claim, and the shorter timeline that can apply, is covered in the wrongful death filing guide and the premises wrongful death overview. Preserve the scene. A repaired rail the next morning is a different case to prove.

For non-fatal negligence claims arising after March 24, 2023, section 95.11 generally allows two years. Comparative fault under section 768.81 will be explored if someone climbed, sat, or leaned in a way the defense will call misuse. A guard that fails under ordinary use is a different fact from a person who climbed it. The photos should show which one happened.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Who has to maintain a condo balcony?

It depends on the declaration. Some balconies are limited common elements the association must structurally maintain. Some repair duties are assigned to the unit owner. The governing documents, not the view, answer that question.

Q: What about an apartment or vacation rental?

Section 83.51, Florida Statutes, sets landlord maintenance duties, including code compliance and, where the statute applies, structural components and the safe condition of common areas. A private balcony has to be matched to the right duty and the lease. On a short-term rental, look at the owner, the host, and any local manager. The booking platform usually does not repair the guard.

Q: What should be photographed?

The guard itself: corrosion, loose posts, missing glass, and any furniture placed against it. Also photograph the door track and the walking surface. Salt air in Miami Beach and along the bay makes deterioration a maintenance issue, not a surprise.
