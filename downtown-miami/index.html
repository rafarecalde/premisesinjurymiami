Downtown Miami and PortMiami Visitor Injuries

Downtown Miami mixes cruise terminals, office towers, public sidewalks, garages, and privately run waterfront. A fall on terminal property is not the same claim as an injury aboard a ship, and a city sidewalk is not the same claim as a private lobby.

The first question is control. Port and terminal floors, curbs, and garages can be maintained by a public agency, a terminal operator, or a contractor. A shipboard injury can follow different rules and a different clock than a fall while you were still on terminal property. The PortMiami and cruise-terminal guide separates those settings. It does not turn every port visit into a maritime case, and it does not turn every shipboard injury into an ordinary premises claim.

Government Property and Section 768.28

Cities, Miami-Dade County, the state, and many public agencies are sued under section 768.28. That statute adds written notice, a waiting period before suit, and damages caps. The notice is not optional paperwork you can send whenever the lawsuit is ready. Sidewalks, public buildings, and some garages downtown fall in this category only when a government defendant actually controlled the spot. The guide explains how to tell a public sidewalk from the private property beside it: sidewalk trip and fall.

Towers, Garages, and the Street

Downtown office lobbies, elevators, and parking structures raise the same ownership split as Brickell. See office building injuries, elevators and escalators, and parking garages. Rideshare and taxi pickup zones at curbs and porte-cocheres are covered in pickup-zone injuries. The property claim is about the curb, the lane, and the lighting. It is not automatically a claim against the driver.

Retail floors and food halls inside downtown buildings still use section 768.0755 when the hazard is a transitory substance. Construction fencing, sidewalk closures, and debris at the edge of a job site are a different problem, covered in construction-site visitor injuries.

Marinas and the Waterfront Edge

Finger piers, public seawalls, and private marina docks fail in ordinary ways: broken planks, dark walks, and unmarked gaps. Who owns the dock decides whether section 768.28 applies. The marina and dock guide and the waterfront and storm pages collect those facts, including debris and dark stairwells after a storm has already passed.

What to Do the Same Day

Photograph the terminal floor, curb, or sidewalk before it is cleaned. If a government agency may be involved, do not wait to ask whether a notice letter is required. For private defendants, most negligence claims arising after March 24, 2023, have two years under section 95.11. Comparative fault under section 768.81 still applies. A free consultation is the right time to sort which clock you are on.

Photograph the condition, keep your shoes, and call before the video is overwritten. Free consultation.
