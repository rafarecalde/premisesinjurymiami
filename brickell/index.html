Brickell Condo, Lobby, and High-Rise Injuries

Brickell is a grid of condominium towers, office lobbies, restaurant floors, and valet lanes. Rain blows into entries. Air conditioning drips onto polished tile. Cruise-day crowds track water through ground-floor restaurants. The claim depends on who controlled that exact spot.

A fall in a Brickell tower is rarely "the building" as a single defendant. The landowner, a condominium or homeowners association, a property manager, a commercial tenant, and an elevator or cleaning contractor can each have a separate duty and a separate policy. Property-management liability and condo and HOA common areas are the two splits that come up most often.

Lobbies, Retail Floors, and Rain

For a spill, tracked-in rain, or a leak that just appeared in a business open to the public, section 768.0755 requires proof of actual or constructive knowledge. A torn mat that stays down, a cracked tile, or a raised threshold is a different kind of hazard. The slip-and-fall pages collect the store, restaurant, and stairway guides that apply inside these towers and along the street.

Office-lobby, garage, and elevator injuries in Brickell and downtown towers are covered in the office building guide and the elevator and escalator guide. The maintenance contractor is often a separate company from the owner on the deed.

Apartments, Condos, and Short-Term Rentals

Stairwells, handrails, and lighting in residential common areas are discussed in apartments, condos, and rentals. Florida landlords have statutory maintenance duties, including common areas, under section 83.51. A short-term rental inside a Brickell condominium adds another layer: the host, the unit owner, and the association may not be the same person. See vacation-rental injuries and balcony and railing falls.

Security at a multifamily building is not the same analysis as security at a ground-floor nightclub. Section 768.0706 is a presumption for certain apartment communities. It does not apply to stores. Lighting, locks, and cameras in a residential garage are discussed under negligent security.

Valet and the Garage

Restaurant and condominium valet stands put people in a drive lane for a handoff. Valet claims and parking-garage claims ask who owned the stall, who ran the stand, and whether the pedestrian path was marked. If the garage is a city or county facility, section 768.28 adds a notice step that private garages do not have.

Deadlines

Most private negligence claims that arose after March 24, 2023, have two years under section 95.11. Lobby cameras are often on a short loop. Photograph the condition, keep the shoes, and get the incident report the day it happens. Do not give a recorded statement to the building insurer first.
