Ports, Marinas, and Property After a Storm

Part of the Miami-Dade premises injury library from Recalde Law Firm, P.A.

Call 305-792-9100 Free Consultation

Miami's waterfront concentrates visitors on property that several parties may control at once: the county, a terminal operator, a marina, a restaurant tenant, a yacht club. Salt air and standing water make deferred maintenance visible, and storms rearrange walkways overnight.

An injury on terminal pavement or inside a cruise terminal, before anyone boards a ship, is often a Florida premises question about whoever controlled that space. If that party is Miami-Dade County or another government entity, section 768.28 applies. An injury aboard a cruise ship can be governed by maritime law and the ticket contract, which may set a shorter time to sue than the two-year period in section 95.11. Do not assume one deadline covers both.

A storm does not, by itself, decide a later fall on a private walkway or in a dark stairwell. Reasonable care after the weather passes depends on who controlled the property, what they knew, and how obvious the danger was. Comparative fault under section 768.81 can also be part of that analysis.
