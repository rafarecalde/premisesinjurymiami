Children, Older Adults, and Wrongful Death

Part of the Miami-Dade premises injury library from Recalde Law Firm, P.A.

The same property defect does not injure every person the same way. A missing handrail that a younger adult grabs and recovers from can produce a hip fracture for an older resident. A playground surface that is too thin, or equipment that is broken, is a hazard aimed at children. A parent who falls in a school lobby is a visitor, and that claim is about the premises, not about classroom supervision.

Public schools, public parks, and county hospitals can be government defendants under section 768.28. Private daycares and private playgrounds at apartment communities are ordinary premises claims. Florida tolls a minor's claim only in narrow situations. Families should not assume a child's deadline waits until adulthood. The playground guide discusses section 95.051 on that point.

When a premises injury is fatal, the claim is brought under Florida's Wrongful Death Act, sections 768.16 through 768.26. Who may file, and who counts as a survivor, is addressed in the guides below. The filing period is generally shorter than people expect.
