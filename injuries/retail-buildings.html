Stores, Malls, Offices, and Warehouses

Part of the Miami-Dade premises injury library from Recalde Law Firm, P.A.

Commercial properties in Miami are layered. A mall owner, a management company, a store tenant, and an escalator contractor can all have a piece of the same fall. The lease and the maintenance contract are how those pieces are identified.

Customer falls on spills inside a business are often governed by section 768.0755. Mechanical failures — an escalator that stops hard, a dock leveler, a piece of gym equipment — are investigated through inspection tags and service records. A person hurt while visiting a warehouse or walking past a construction site is in a different posture from an employee of that site, whose injury may be a workers' compensation matter against their own employer.

Brickell and downtown office lobbies, Aventura and Dadeland malls, and the warehouse corridors in Doral and Medley show up for the same reason: polished floors, shared common areas, and vendors who assumed someone else was watching the hazard.
