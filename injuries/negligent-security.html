Negligent Security on Miami Property

Part of the Miami-Dade premises injury library from Recalde Law Firm, P.A.

A negligent security claim is a premises claim about a crime committed by someone else. The question is whether the person who controlled the property failed to use reasonable care — lighting, locks, gates, cameras, or staffing — in the face of a foreseeable risk, and whether that failure contributed to the injury.

Two statutes matter, and they are easy to mix up. Section 768.0706 is a presumption for certain multifamily residential properties of at least five units. It is not a rule for nightclubs, gas stations, or malls. Section 768.0701 requires the jury, in a claim against a property owner or operator by a person lawfully on the property who was hurt by a third party's crime, to consider the fault of everyone who contributed, including the person who committed the crime. Florida's comparative fault bar in section 768.81 still applies.

Video, prior incident reports, work orders for broken gates, and the lighting actually present that night are the records that disappear first. A criminal case against the attacker is a separate proceeding from the civil claim against the property.
